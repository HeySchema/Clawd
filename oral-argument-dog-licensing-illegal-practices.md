# Oral Argument -- Illegal Practices in Berkeley Township Dog Licensing Enforcement

**In the Matter of:** Dog Licensing Violation, Berkeley Township Municipal Court
**Subject:** Chapter 9, Part I -- Animal Control, Township of Berkeley, Ocean County, NJ

---

Your Honor, I respectfully ask this Court to dismiss the summons issued against me, or in the alternative, to limit any penalty to the range authorized by New Jersey state law. Berkeley Township's dog licensing ordinance, as written and as enforced, exceeds the municipality's authority in multiple respects and cannot be sustained against the controlling provisions of N.J.S.A. 4:19-15 et seq.

**First, the penalties are illegal.** The ordinance subjects violators to a fine of up to $500 and imprisonment of up to 90 days. State law says otherwise. N.J.S.A. 4:19-15.19 through 4:19-15.23 set the penalty for a first licensing offense at $1 to $50, and confinement for non-payment at no more than 10 days. The Township's $500 maximum is ten times the statutory ceiling. Its 90-day imprisonment provision is nine times what the Legislature authorized for a first offense. While municipalities possess general penalty authority under N.J.S.A. 40:49-5, it is settled law that general authority does not override specific penalty ceilings established by subject-matter statutes. Where the Legislature has spoken specifically on the penalties for dog licensing violations, that specific range controls, and this ordinance exceeds it.

**Second, the compounding daily fines violate due process.** The ordinance declares that "each day's failure to obtain a license is deemed a separate violation." This transforms a single continuing omission -- not having a license -- into an endlessly multiplying series of offenses. A resident unaware of the requirement could accumulate thousands of dollars in fines before receiving any notice. State law imposes a single fine for the offense of failure to license. It does not authorize daily compounding. This provision offends both the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment and the Excessive Fines Clause of the Eighth Amendment, as well as their counterparts in the New Jersey Constitution at Article I, Paragraphs 1 and 12.

**Third, the ordinance's own foundation is defective.** The ordinance cites the Animal Control Officer's enforcement authority as deriving from "N.J.S.A. 4:19-15 through 4:10-15.29." The citation "4:10-15.29" does not exist. The correct reference is 4:19-15.29. While this Court may regard this as a scrivener's error, it nonetheless calls into question the precision and care with which this ordinance was drafted -- an ordinance that now seeks to impose penalties far beyond what Trenton has authorized.

**Fourth, state preemption limits what this municipality can do.** N.J.S.A. 4:19-36 expressly provides that the state's dog control statutes supersede any local ordinance that is inconsistent with state law. The New Jersey courts have confirmed this preemption, holding in *State v. Smith* that municipal ordinances with independent procedural features for dog-related enforcement conflict with the state Act and deprive the municipal court of subject matter jurisdiction. To the extent Berkeley Township's penalty provisions, daily compounding fines, and enforcement procedures deviate from the state framework, they are preempted and void.

Your Honor, I am not here to argue that dog licensing is unnecessary. I accept my obligation to license my dog under state law, and I intend to comply. But compliance with the law is a two-way street. This Township cannot impose fines ten times the legal limit, threaten 90 days of imprisonment where the state authorizes 10, stack daily penalties for a single omission, and then tell its residents that it is acting within the law. It is not.

I respectfully ask this Court to recognize the state penalty caps, dismiss any charges premised on the illegal penalty provisions, and limit any fine to the $1 to $50 range that the Legislature of this State has determined is appropriate.

Thank you, Your Honor.
